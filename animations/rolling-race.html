The rolling race HL

A hoop, a solid disc and a solid ball roll without slipping down the same slope. Which wins, and why does the shape matter but not the mass or the size? A.4 Rigid body mechanics

What the animation shows

  1. A hoop ($I = mr^2$), a solid disc ($I = \tfrac{1}{2}mr^2$) and a solid ball ($I = \tfrac{2}{5}mr^2$), all with the same mass and radius, start from rest at the top of identical slopes, 0.50 m high at 20° (so each slope is $L = h/\sin\theta = 1.46$ m long). You are asked to predict which reaches the bottom first.
  2. They are released together and roll without slipping, shown four times slower than real life. The marks on each object turn as it rolls: its centre moves at $v = \omega r$.
  3. The ball wins in 1.11 s. The disc is close behind at 1.14 s, and the hoop is last at 1.32 s.
  4. Why: each object loses the same gravitational potential energy, $mgh$. At the bottom it is shared between moving along, $\tfrac{1}{2}mv^2$ (blue bars), and spinning, $\tfrac{1}{2}I\omega^2$ (gold bars), so $mgh = \tfrac{1}{2}mv^2 + \tfrac{1}{2}I\omega^2$. The race runs again with live energy bars.
  5. The hoop has all its mass at the rim, so half of its energy goes into spinning: $v^2 = gh$, so $v = 2.21\ \text{m s}^{-1}$.
  6. The disc keeps two-thirds of its energy for moving along and spins with one-third: $v^2 = \tfrac{4}{3}gh$, so $v = 2.56\ \text{m s}^{-1}$.
  7. The ball spins with only $\tfrac{2}{7}$ of its energy (71% moving along, 29% spinning): $v^2 = \tfrac{10}{7}gh$, so $v = 2.65\ \text{m s}^{-1}$. Its mass is closest to the centre, so it has the smallest $I$ for its mass and radius, and wins.
  8. A surprise: a big hoop with 4 times the mass and 3 times the radius races a small, light hoop. The small hoop spins 3 times as fast ($\omega = v/r$), but they stay side by side, and they tie at 1.32 s. In $mgh = \tfrac{1}{2}mv^2 + \tfrac{1}{2}(mr^2)(v/r)^2$ the mass and the radius cancel, so $v^2 = gh$ for every hoop.
  9. So only the shape matters: how the mass is spread out around the axis. The animation ends with $E_k = \tfrac{1}{2}mv^2 + \tfrac{1}{2}I\omega^2$ and $v = \omega r$: the more of the mass is far from the axis, the larger $I$ is compared with $mr^2$, and the slower the object rolls.

The times come from the acceleration down the slope, $a = \dfrac{g\sin\theta}{1 + I/(mr^2)}$, with $g = 9.8\ \text{m s}^{-2}$, and $t = 2L/v$ for a start from rest. Read more in A.4 section 9: rotational kinetic energy and rolling and section 5: moment of inertia.
