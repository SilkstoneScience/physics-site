Animations

Short animations that play by themselves: watch an idea unfold one step at a time. Each one loops: click or tap the picture to pause it, drag the bar along the bottom (or press ← and →) to go back or forward, use 1× to play it at 1.5 or 2 times the speed, and use Full screen for the board. Each has its own background music, made by your browser as it plays; browsers start sound only after a first click or tap, and the ♪ Music button turns it off (your choice is remembered on this device). Every animation also describes its steps in words underneath.

Physics skills

  • Powers of ten

    Zoom out from a proton to the observable universe: about 42 orders of magnitude.

Theme A: Space, time and motion

  • A.1 Motion graphs

    A car speeds up, stops and reverses while its s–t, v–t and a–t graphs draw themselves.

  • A.2 What exactly is friction?

    From a block that won't budge to the atoms at the surfaces: bumps, real contact spots, and bonds that form and break.

  • A.2 Collisions, explosions and rockets

    Momentum before and after elastic and sticky collisions and an explosion, then how a rocket moves by throwing gas backwards.

  • A.3 A bungee jump

    Energy flows between gravitational, kinetic and elastic stores, then into thermal energy, while the total stays the same.

  • A.4 Why the sin θ in torque? HL

    The force's perpendicular component, or the perpendicular distance to its line of action? Both, then a ladder against a wall.

  • A.4 The rolling race HL

    A hoop, a disc and a ball race down a slope. Which wins, and why doesn't mass matter?

  • A.5 Space–time diagrams HL

    World lines, the tilted axes of a moving frame, simultaneity and time dilation.

Theme B: The particulate nature of matter

  • B.1 Heating ice to steam

    What the molecules do as ice warms, melts, boils and becomes steam, with a heating curve drawn to scale.

  • B.3 Where gas pressure comes from

    Molecules hitting the walls make the pressure. Halve the volume, or double the temperature, and it doubles.

  • B.4 The first law and heat engines HL

    A gas in a cylinder goes through the four kinds of process beside its p–V diagram, with the heat, internal energy and work to scale, then a Carnot engine runs between 500 K and 300 K.

  • B.4 Entropy and the second law HL

    A gas spreads out and never gathers back: count the microstates, turn them into entropy, and see why heat flows from hot to cold.

  • B.5 Inside a wire

    Free electrons, a slow drift, collisions that cause resistance, and why the lamp lights at once.

Theme C: Wave behaviour

  • C.1 Simple harmonic motion

    A glider oscillates between springs as its x–t, v–t and a–t graphs draw themselves, the energy swaps between kinetic and potential, and (HL) a point on a circle keeps in step.

  • C.2 Transverse and longitudinal waves

    Follow one particle as each kind of wave passes, turn a longitudinal wave into a graph, and see why v = fλ.

  • C.3 Wavefronts and rays

    Refraction into shallow water, diffraction through a wide and a narrow gap, and the interference pattern of two sources, in a ripple tank.

  • C.4 Standing waves

    Two waves travelling opposite ways make a wave that stays put. Then the harmonics of a string, and the air moving inside open and closed pipes.

  • C.5 The Doppler effect

    Wavefronts bunch up ahead of a moving car and spread out behind, a driver races towards a siren, and a galaxy's light is redshifted.

Theme D: Fields

  • D.1 Gravitational field lines

    The Earth's field weakens with distance, is almost uniform at the surface, and cancels with the Moon's 90% of the way there; (HL) equipotentials cross the field lines at right angles.

  • D.1 The gravitational potential well HL

    A graph of potential spun into a surface: a satellite circles, climbs to a higher orbit and escapes, while its energies change.

  • D.2 Electric field lines and equipotentials

    Calculated field lines around point charges, pairs of charges and parallel plates, with the force on a test charge; then (HL) equipotentials at right angles to them.

  • D.3 A charge in a magnetic field

    Why a moving charge goes round in a circle, why opposite charges curve opposite ways, and when the path is a helix.

  • D.3 Charges in electric and crossed fields

    An electron bent into a parabola between charged plates, a velocity selector that lets through one speed, and ions sorted by charge-to-mass ratio.

  • D.4 Induction HL

    A magnet pushed into, pulled out of and dropped through a coil, with live graphs of flux linkage and emf, Lenz's law, and an ac generator.

Theme E: Nuclear and quantum physics

  • E.1 Rutherford scattering

    Alpha particles hit a gold foil: most pass straight through, a few bounce back. Calculated paths past a single nucleus show why, and (HL) how close a head-on alpha particle gets.

  • E.1 Energy levels and spectra

    An electron jumps between hydrogen's energy levels, drawn to scale, and each photon it gives out becomes a line in the spectrum; then white light through cooler gas gives dark lines.

  • E.2 The photoelectric effect HL

    Red, violet and ultraviolet light on a sodium photocell: one photon frees one electron, the stopping voltage measures Eₘₐₓ, and the Eₘₐₓ–f graph gives h and Φ.

  • E.3 Random decay and half-life

    1000 nuclei decay at random while a graph counts how many are left: about half go every half-life, but a sample of 20 is far less predictable.

  • E.4 Fission and the chain reaction

    A uranium nucleus splits, a chain reaction spreads through a lump of uranium, a small lump shows why there is a critical mass, and a reactor's control rods keep it steady.

  • E.5 The HR diagram and how stars evolve

    Real stars placed on an HR diagram, lines of constant radius, and the approximate paths of a Sun-like star and a 25-solar-mass star as they age.
