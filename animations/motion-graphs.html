Motion graphs

A car speeds up, cruises, stops, reverses and stops again, while its three motion graphs draw themselves. Then the areas and gradients that link the graphs. A.1 Kinematics

What the animation shows

The car starts at rest at $s = 0$. To the right is positive. The green dots on the track show where the car was at each whole second: far apart means fast.

  1. 0–4 s: it speeds up at $1.5$ $\text{m s}^{-2}$ to $6$ $\text{m s}^{-1}$. The s–t graph curves upwards and the v–t graph is a straight line sloping up.
  2. 4–8 s: constant velocity, $6$ $\text{m s}^{-1}$. The s–t graph is straight and the acceleration is zero.
  3. 8–11 s: it slows down at $2$ $\text{m s}^{-2}$ and stops at $s = 45$ m. The acceleration is negative.
  4. 11–13 s: at rest. The s–t graph is flat and the velocity is zero.
  5. 13–16 s: it speeds up backwards to $-3$ $\text{m s}^{-1}$. The velocity is negative, so the displacement decreases.
  6. 16–19 s: constant velocity backwards.
  7. 19–20.5 s: it slows down while moving backwards, so its acceleration is positive ($+2$ $\text{m s}^{-2}$).
  8. The area under the v–t graph is the displacement: $45$ m forwards (above the axis) and $15.75$ m back (below it), so it ends at $45 - 15.75 = 29.25$ m.
  9. The gradient of the s–t graph is the velocity (at 9.5 s it is $3$ $\text{m s}^{-1}$). The gradient of the v–t graph is the acceleration ($6 \div 4 = 1.5$ $\text{m s}^{-2}$ in the first 4 s).
