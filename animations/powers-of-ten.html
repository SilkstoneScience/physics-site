Powers of ten

Zoom out from a proton to the observable universe. At every moment, the size of the picture is shown as a power of ten, and each object is drawn at its real size for that view (except stars and planets shown as dots, which would be far too small to see). Physics skills: orders of magnitude

Everything else is drawn for this site. The Hubble photo shows galaxies in every direction; it is not drawn to the scale of the view.

What the animation shows

Each step on the way out makes the picture ten times taller. The sizes, roughly:

  1. A proton: $1.7 \times 10^{-15}$ m across. It is made of three quarks.
  2. An atom: about $10^{-10}$ m across, about 60 000 times wider than a proton. Its nucleus is 10 000 to 100 000 times smaller than the atom.
  3. DNA: about $2 \times 10^{-9}$ m wide, about 20 times wider than an atom.
  4. A virus: about $10^{-7}$ m across, about 50 times wider than DNA.
  5. A red blood cell: about $8 \times 10^{-6}$ m across, about 80 times wider than a virus.
  6. A human hair: about $8 \times 10^{-5}$ m wide, about 10 times wider than a red blood cell.
  7. A raindrop: about $2 \times 10^{-3}$ m across, about 25 times wider than a hair.
  8. A person: about $1.7$ m tall, about 850 times a raindrop's width.
  9. Mount Everest: $8.8 \times 10^{3}$ m high, about 5000 times a person's height.
  10. Earth: $1.27 \times 10^{7}$ m across, about 1400 times the height of Everest.
  11. The Sun: $1.39 \times 10^{9}$ m across, 109 times wider than Earth; about 1.3 million Earths would fit inside it.
  12. The orbits of the planets: Neptune's orbit is about $9 \times 10^{12}$ m across, about 6500 times the Sun's width.
  13. The nearest star after the Sun, Proxima Centauri: $4.0 \times 10^{16}$ m away (4.2 light-years), about 4400 times the width of Neptune's orbit. A pulse of light is shown crossing the gap.
  14. Our galaxy, the Milky Way: about $10^{21}$ m across (100 000 light-years), about 25 000 times the distance to the nearest star.
  15. The Andromeda galaxy: $2.4 \times 10^{22}$ m away (2.5 million light-years), about 24 times the width of our galaxy.
  16. The observable universe: about $8.8 \times 10^{26}$ m across. It is about 37 000 times the distance to Andromeda. It is the part of the universe we can see, so it is centred on us. Space has expanded while the light travelled, so its edge is now about 46 billion light-years away, not 13.8 billion.

From a proton to the observable universe is about 42 orders of magnitude: $\dfrac{8.8 \times 10^{26}}{1.7 \times 10^{-15}} \approx 5 \times 10^{41}$. See Physics skills for orders of magnitude and standard form.
