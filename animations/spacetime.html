Space–time diagrams HL

How a space–time diagram shows the same events for the ground and for a rocket moving at $0.60c$: world lines, tilted axes, simultaneity and time dilation. A.5 Galilean and special relativity

What the animation shows

  1. The axes are $x$ across and $ct$ up, both in metres. Each point is an event. An object at rest has a vertical world line.
  2. Light has a world line at 45°.
  3. A rocket moving at $0.60c$ has a world line tilted by $\theta$ from the $ct$ axis, where $\tan\theta = v/c = 0.60$, so $\theta = 31°$.
  4. The rocket's frame S′ has its own axes: $ct'$ along the rocket's world line and $x'$ tilted up from the $x$ axis by the same angle. The light line is still halfway between them, so light has speed $c$ in both frames.
  5. Events A and B are on a line parallel to the $x$ axis, so they are simultaneous for the ground. Lines through them parallel to the $x'$ axis meet the $ct'$ axis at different heights: for the rocket, B happens first ($ct'_B = 1.6$ m, $ct'_A = 3.4$ m).
  6. Curves of equal space–time interval, $(ct)^2 - x^2$, cross the $ct$ axis at 1 m, 2 m, 3 m… and the $ct'$ axis at $ct' =$ 1 m, 2 m, 3 m…, so they show the scale on both time axes.
  7. The rocket's clock reads $ct' = 2$ m at the event where the ground's clock reads $ct = 2.5$ m: the moving clock runs slow by the factor $\gamma = 1.25$.
