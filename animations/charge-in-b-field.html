A charge in a magnetic field

Why a charged particle moving across a uniform magnetic field goes round in a circle, and when its path becomes a helix. D.3 Motion in electromagnetic fields

What the animation shows

  1. A uniform magnetic field points into the page (the crosses). A positive charge moves to the right.
  2. The magnetic force (orange) is always at right angles to the velocity (blue). It changes the direction of motion but never the speed, so the charge moves in a circle, anticlockwise for this field.
  3. A negative charge with the same mass and speed, launched the same way, feels a force in the opposite direction and curves the other way.
  4. The view turns so the field lines are seen from the side.
  5. A charge that also has some velocity along the field feels no force from that part of its motion, so it keeps drifting along the field while it circles: its path is a helix.
  6. The size of the force is $F = qvB\sin\theta$, where $\theta$ is the angle between the velocity and the field.

The paths are calculated from the force on the charge, not drawn by hand. Read more in D.3 section 2: the magnetic force on a moving charge and section 3: circular motion in a magnetic field.
