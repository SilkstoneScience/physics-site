Review games

Revise by playing. Every game uses real physics from the syllabus, and most of them show you what to revise next. Your progress is saved only in this browser.

Teachers: teaching notes for the mysteries (timings, lesson plans and how to run them with groups) are on the For teachers page.

  • Physics escape room

    Choose the topics, then open each lock with a multiple-choice question and collect the exit code. Race the clock, or play as a class on the board.

  • Physics mystery: The vanishing meteorite

    A meteorite disappears from a museum on a stormy night. Test each suspect's alibi with a calculation, then write your accusation. Works well in groups. SL, about 30–50 minutes.

    A.1 B.1 B.5 C.2

  • Physics mystery: The crime scene at Fenwick Hall

    A professor is drugged and a priceless carving vanishes during a dinner party. Search the crime scene, test the alibis and write your accusation. Works well in groups. SL, about 30–50 minutes.

    A.2 C.1 C.3 E.3

  • Physics mystery: The flywheel at Ravensby

    A priceless notebook vanishes from a dark archive during an evening talk. Two alarms stayed silent and a giant flywheel was still turning. HL, about 30–50 minutes.

    A.4 B.4 D.4 E.2 HL

  • Physics newsroom

    You're the science editor. Find the physics errors in made-up news stories, adverts and reviews, say what's wrong, and rewrite each sentence. About 10 minutes per article.

  • Explanation builder

    Build a full-mark answer from sentence pieces, leaving out the vague and wrong ones. Each question ends with an examiner's tip.

  • Estimation challenges

    How much does the air in your classroom weigh? How long will the Sun shine? State your assumptions, make an estimate, and compare your reasoning with ours.

  • Word games

    Quick games for the start or end of a lesson: Forbidden words, Odd one out, and a crossword that you can print.
