## Question bank: Themes A and B

**T1** · A1-014 (optional). The answer ("lower and shorter in air") is right for an object that feels only air resistance, which is what the syllabus means. But a real golf ball is hit with backspin, and the lift this gives means a real drive goes higher and further in air than it would in a vacuum. A well-read student could object. Changing the object removes the objection; the question is defensible as it stands.

**T2** · A2-023 (a), feedback messages (optional). Two automatic feedback messages for common wrong answers could be clearer. For 1.667 (the student left out P's final momentum altogether) the message only talks about the sign. For 1.333 the message says P's momentum "must be subtracted … not added", which a student who wrote 1.5 − 0.30 can read as the opposite of what they need. The values themselves are right, and the present wording is not actually wrong, so I rate this lower than the first reviewer did: a wording improvement, not an error.

**T3** · B1-002 (optional). The question asks for "the average kinetic energy of a molecule in a gas" and the answer uses (3/2)k_BT, which is strictly the average *translational* kinetic energy. The notes page was changed to say "translational" in the last audit (decision D8). The 2025 guide itself says "average kinetic energy of particles", so the question is defensible; adding one word matches the notes.

**T4** · B1-020. Statements I and II of this question test exactly the two "synonymous" statements (luminosity = radiated power; apparent brightness = intensity) that you chose to keep tagged "New for 2028" on the B.1 page (decision D9). The question has no "New for 2028" tag. The question-bank rule in CLAUDE.md counts only I = P/A as new for B.1, so under that rule it is correctly untagged; under D9 it would be tagged. The page and the question bank currently treat the same two statements differently. The physics and the answer are right either way.

**T5** · B4-013 (d). The question asks for "the efficiency" without saying decimal or percentage, and the next part talks of "67%". A student who types 10 (for 10%) is told "Not quite"; one who types 10% is told the number can't be read. The stored answer is the decimal 0.1.

**T6** · B5-010 (optional). The question doesn't say which type of thermistor. The answer is right for an NTC thermistor (resistance falls when hotter), which is the type the notes say the IB uses; for a PTC thermistor option A would be right. Adding "NTC" removes the dependence on that convention. This is a clarification, not a correction, and IB questions usually say just "thermistor", so leaving it is equally defensible.


## Question bank: Themes C, D and E

**T7** · D4-011. This question can only be answered with self-induction. The 2025 guide requires it qualitatively; the 2028 guide deletes that sentence. The answer is right and the question is valid up to 2027, but nothing tells a 2028 student that it is no longer in the guide. `"y2028": true` would be wrong (it shows "New for 2028"; this is a removal), and the question format has no "changed for 2028" field.

**T8** · C4-013. The stem says the column "resonates at two lengths that differ by 0.39 m" without saying they are neighbouring resonances. If the two were one resonance apart from neighbouring (for example the first and the third), option B (172 m s⁻¹) would be right. One word fixes it; it is a stem change, so it is the teacher's call.

**T9** · C4-015 (optional). The explanation rejects option B with "Nodes don't move at all, so they have no energy to transfer". Strictly, a node has no kinetic energy but the string is stretched most there, so the stored (potential) energy is greatest at the nodes. However, the site's own notes take the simple view too, so changing only the question would make it disagree with the page. The answer (A) is right either way.

**T10** · D4-003 (optional). The stem says the rod "moves at right angles to" the field but not that the rod itself is at right angles to both. That is the guide's and the notes' usual phrasing, so it is defensible as it is; the change only tightens it.

**T11** · D1-001 (b) mark scheme (optional). The mark scheme substitutes the rounded 7.7 × 10³ m s⁻¹, gets 5.5 × 10³ s, then says "≈ 93 minutes"; 5.5 × 10³ s is 92 minutes and the substitution really gives 92.5. "Accept 92 to 93" covers it, so this is tidying only. I disagree with the first reviewer's fix (changing 5.5 to 5.6 while keeping 7.7 × 10³): that line would then not give its own result (2π × 6.8e6 / 7.7e3 = 5549 s).

**T12** · C1-018 (c) (optional). A student who carries ω = 16 rad s⁻¹ from part (a) gets 1.108 m s⁻¹. Typing 1.1 or 1.108 is marked right, but 1.11 is marked wrong (2.02 % from the stored 1.088). The mark scheme for (c) doesn't mention ECF, so this is optional.

**T13** · E1-003 (optional). The key is 401 nm (using hc = 1.99 × 10⁻²⁵ J m). The booklet's other form, hc = 1.24 × 10⁻⁶ eV m, gives exactly 400 nm, which isn't listed, though 401 nm is plainly the nearest. Nothing is wrong.

**T14** · E2-009 (optional). The explanation says the photoelectric effect, Compton scattering and line emission spectra are all "evidence for photons". Line spectra are normally quoted (and in the guide, E.1) as evidence for discrete energy levels. The answer (electron diffraction) is unaffected.

**T15** · E5-013 (optional). "Where are most elements heavier than iron produced?" has the expected answer D (supernovae and neutron-star collisions), which matches the notes. But about half of the heavier-than-iron nuclei (for example most barium, strontium and lead) are made by slow neutron capture in giant stars of roughly Sun-like mass, so option B ("In red giant stars like the future Sun") is not cleanly wrong for a well-read student. This is beyond the syllabus, so whether to tighten the distractor is the teacher's call.


## Skills page, Newsroom, Explanation builder and word games

**T16** · skills.html. The page's own rule (line 180) says a value $a\times10^{n}$ rounds up to $10^{n+1}$ when $a$ is above about 3.16. The speed of sound, 340 = 3.4 × 10², is above that, so the rule gives 10³, but the table says 10². A student applying the rule to the table gets a different answer.

**T17** · skills.html. Same contradiction. 400–700 nm is 4 × 10⁻⁷ to 7 × 10⁻⁷ m; every value in the range has a first number above 3.16, so the page's rule gives 10⁻⁶ m, but the table says 10⁻⁷ m.

**T18** · skills.html. The table shows one year as 3.2 × 10⁷ s with order 10⁷ s. As printed, 3.2 is above 3.16, so the rule would give 10⁸. The real value (3.156 × 10⁷ s) is a whisker below the cut-off, so 10⁷ is right, but the rounded figure hides that. It is the most borderline example possible.

**T19** · skills.html (optional). N is drawn left of the crate's centre. With a rope pulling at the top-right corner and friction dragging back along the bottom, the floor's push actually acts right of centre (that is what stops the crate tipping forwards). This is beyond what the page teaches, so it only matters if the arrow is being redrawn anyway.

**T20** · skills.html (optional). Line 532 says a gradient is a rate "only when x is time", but line 281 defines a rate of change as how fast one quantity changes compared with another, "usually time". The two lines don't quite agree. Many teachers do keep the word "rate" for time, so this is a wording choice.

**T21** · skills.html. The sentence says all the conversions in the table are in the data booklet. The booklet's list has the radian, kelvin, light year, parsec, AU, kWh and hc only. Hour, day and year are not there, and the eV appears only through the elementary charge in the constants table.

**T22** · skills.html. The key box writes the components as $A_x$ and $A_y$; the data booklet (and the site's own Σ panel) writes $A_H$ and $A_V$. A one-line note is simpler than renaming every use in the section.

**T23** · skills.html (optional). The page's rule (line 415) says an uncertainty from half a scale division gets 1 significant figure, but this worked example writes (12.5 ± 1.0) °C, which is 2. Writing ± 1 would force 12.5 to be rounded, which is awkward, so the cleanest cure changes one reading. Many teachers and mark schemes accept 12.5 ± 1.0, so this is the teacher's call.

**T24** · skills.html. The line above the table says the prefixes are "given in the data booklet", but the booklet lists 14 and the table shows 11. Hecto, deca and deci are missing. Adding rows is new content, so it is the teacher's choice.

**T25** · skills.html. Two small items from the booklet's maths page are not on the Skills page: the volume of a prism, V = Ah (it is in the site's Σ panel), and the kelvin conversion. Both are optional additions.

**T26** · titan (optional). The corrected sentence says the siren "drops in pitch" as the rover drives away. For a rover moving away at a steady speed the pitch heard is lower than the true pitch but steady; it doesn't keep falling. "Drops" can be read as "is lower", so this is a wording preference, but the reworded version avoids feeding a common misconception.

**T27** · exoplanet (optional). A sentence the game treats as correct says "a quarter of the solar constant at that distance" for a planet of another star. The notes (B.2, line 151) define the solar constant as the Sun's intensity at the Earth's distance, so a careful student could click this sentence and lose points. The physics (the S/4 rule) is right; only the term is loose.

**T28** · spin-cycle (optional). One piece of feedback says the skater's muscles "change how [angular momentum] is shared between I and ω". Angular momentum is the product Iω, not something shared out between the two. The rest of the feedback is right; this is a tidier wording.

**T29** · EX-A1-02 (optional). Same pattern: "the gradient of a displacement–time graph is the velocity" and "the graph gets steeper, so the gradient increases" could be written in either order, but the game accepts only one. Here the examiner's tip says "say what the gradient represents before you use it", so the fixed order may be deliberate. The teacher decides.

**T30** · EX-C2-01 (optional). The sentence about light doesn't depend on the two about sound, so "light first" is valid physics but is marked out of order. Step numbers can't express this (the light sentence can't be made swappable with a two-sentence chain), and the present order follows the question (sound fades, bell still seen). **T31** · EX-E3-01 (optional). Same as the bell-jar item: the gamma sentence is independent of the two alpha sentences, so "gamma first" is valid but marked out of order. The order follows the question (alpha, then gamma). Optional rewording so it reads as a contrast.

**T32** · EX-B1-02 (optional). The first sentence talks about equal energy per square metre, the third about "the same energy to the same mass". The question restricts the answer to specific heat capacity, and the tip explains the "hold the same quantity fixed" idea, so this is loose, not wrong. **T33** · EX-E4-01 (optional). The first sentence says each fission releases "several" neutrons. The notes say "two or three" (`themes/e4.html`), and the Newsroom article on the reactor uses the same phrase. "Several" isn't false, but matching the notes is tidier.

**T34** · OO-17. ## Mysteries and Mission log

**T36** · ravensby. The brochure says the investors' meeting is "next Monday", Dr Zhou starts scanning "next Tuesday", and the model answer says the scans "would have exposed him before his investors' meeting". Most readers will put Monday before Tuesday, and then the scans start the day after the meeting, so that sentence is false. The culprit and the rest of the motive are not affected. **T37** · meteorite (optional). **T38** · fenwick (optional). A student who types the wine glass's 1.58 (a guess, not a calculation) is told "use the sines, not the angles", because 1.58 is within 2% of the stored mistake 1.6. Nobody doing the calculation arrives at 1.58, so it is harmless. If wanted, the same one-line fix as the kettle removes it.

**T39** · europa. If a student gets the number wrong twice (or presses "show me the working") and then chooses the right order, the game marks the order green and then shows a failure paragraph that says the opposite order was carried out ("The crew waited", "The rover fell short", "The order was wrong"). This is the usual way to fail, not a rare one, because the student has just been shown the working. The behaviour is intended (the teacher page says a second wrong number counts as a mistake); only the wording of the failure texts is wrong for this path. It can be fixed in the data alone, by rewording the texts so they are true on both paths, as decisions 1, 2 and 10A already are. No code change is needed.

**T40** · europa. Two captain's-log prompts assume the student gave the right order ("why did you start the valve repair without waiting", "why did you wire the heaters in parallel"). A student whose decision went wrong was told something different happened. Rewording them as "why was it right to…" works on every path.

**T41** · europa. Decision 2 gives Jupiter as 5.2 AU from the Sun, and decision 4 puts the ship 6.3 AU from Earth. With those numbers the most it can be is 5.2 + 1.0 = 6.2 AU, and a sharp student will notice. The real Jupiter is sometimes further out than 5.2 AU (I am sure of the range, about 4.95 to 5.46 AU), so 6.3 AU is possible in reality. One added phrase removes the clash without changing any number.

**T42** · europa. Decision 5 says the ship goes round Jupiter once while the lander is away and meets it "at the end of that orbit", and the chosen orbit takes 10.0 days. But the lander undocks on day 660 and docks on day 672, which is 12 days. Moving the undocking and landing two days later makes it exactly 10 days and leaves everything after the landing untouched.

**T43** · ravensby (optional). The Carnot limit is 19.55%. The accepted range is 19.3–19.8, so a student who rounds to 2 significant figures and types 20 is marked wrong with only the general "Not quite" message. The clue's own correct option says "about 20%". Either widen the range or leave it and accept that 3 significant figures are needed.

**T44** · teachers/mission-europa.html (optional). The first reviewer could not confirm the reading count. My count agrees with 3,800 only if both routes and all eight detours are included. One play-through with no detours is about 3,000–3,100 words.
