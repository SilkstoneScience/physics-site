Centripetal acceleration with a pendulum

A lab with Vernier sensors: a force sensor measures the tension in a pendulum's string, and a photogate measures the bob's speed. A.2 Forces and momentum

Aim: to find how the force needed to keep an object moving in a circle depends on its speed and on the radius of the circle, and from this to deduce an equation for its acceleration.

1. Equipment and safety

  • Vernier interface (LabQuest Mini or LabQuest Stream) and a laptop with Logger Pro 3
  • Vernier Dual-Range Force Sensor and Vernier Photogate
  • Ring stand (or support rod and base), a second rod, and a right-angle clamp
  • About 1.2 m of string, a mass hanger and slotted masses
  • Metre rule or tape measure, vernier calipers and a balance

Safety: clamp the stand firmly to the bench so it can't tip over, and keep the area around the swinging bob clear. Check that the slotted masses can't slide off the hanger before each swing.

2. Before the lab

Tie a soft object, such as a rubber stopper, to about 1 m of string. Standing well away from other people, swing it in a horizontal circle above your head at a steady speed. Feel the pull of the string on your hand. Then swing it faster, and then with a shorter string.

In this lab, you treat the path of a swinging pendulum bob as part of a circle, centred on the hook it hangs from. Answer these questions before you start.

3. Setting up the equipment

  1. Clamp the ring stand to the edge of the bench. Fix the second rod horizontally with the right-angle clamp, and attach the force sensor to it so that the sensor hangs vertically with its hook pointing down.
  2. Tie the mass hanger to one end of a 0.7–1.0 m length of string. Tie a loop in the other end and hang it on the force sensor's hook, so that the hanger can swing freely.
  3. Add slotted masses to the hanger. Measure the diameter of the slotted masses (the part that will pass through the photogate) with vernier calipers.
  4. Stand the photogate on the floor under the bob, so that the bob passes between its arms at the bottom of its swing without touching them. Check by swinging the bob gently by hand.
  5. Plug the force sensor into CH 1 and the photogate into DIG 1 on the interface. Set the force sensor's switch to ±10 N.

4. Setting up Logger Pro

  1. Connect the interface to the laptop with its USB cable, then open Logger Pro 3. It should find both sensors: you will see a force reading and the photogate's state at the bottom of the window.
  2. Data collection: choose Experiment ▸ Data Collection… Set the mode to Time Based, the length to 10 seconds and the sampling rate to 50 samples/second. Click Done.
  3. Photogate: choose Experiment ▸ Set Up Sensors ▸ Show All Interfaces. Click the picture of the photogate and choose Gate Timing. Click the picture again, choose Set Distance or Length, and type the diameter of the slotted masses in metres (for example 0.050 for 5.0 cm). Close the window. Logger Pro now calculates the bob's speed each time it passes through the gate.
  4. Zero the force sensor: lift the string off the hook, so that nothing is hanging from it. Choose Experiment ▸ Zero. Then hang the string back on the hook. (Zeroing with nothing hanging means the sensor measures the whole tension in the string.)
  5. Graphs: you need two graphs, force against time and velocity against time. If only one graph shows, choose Insert ▸ Graph. Click the label on the vertical axis of each graph to choose what it shows: Force on one and Velocity on the other.
  6. Test it: pull the bob back a little, release it, and click Collect. You should see a force peak each time the bob passes through the bottom, and a velocity reading each time it passes through the gate.

How to read the maximum values (used in Parts 1 and 2): on the force graph, drag across a section where the force peaks are steady for at least three swings. Choose Analyze ▸ Statistics and read the maximum. Select the same section of the velocity graph, choose Analyze ▸ Statistics again, and read the maximum velocity.

5. Part 1: force and speed

Keep the radius and the mass the same, and change the speed by releasing the bob from different angles.

Measurements for Part 1

  1. Weigh the hanger with its slotted masses. Treat it as a single point mass: its centre of mass is the point where it would balance if laid on its side. Measure the radius from the hook to this point.
  2. Pull the bob back 15–20° from the vertical and release it. If it swings smoothly through the photogate, click Collect. If not, stop it and try again.
  3. Read the maximum force and the maximum velocity with Analyze ▸ Statistics (see the box above). Record them in the table.
  4. Release the bob from a slightly larger angle each time, until you have at least 7 readings. Your largest speed should be at least 4 times your smallest.

6. Part 2: force and radius

Keep the speed and the mass the same, and change the radius by shortening the string.

Measurements for Part 2

  1. Set the radius to at least 0.9 m, and measure it from the hook to the bob's centre of mass.
  2. Release the bob from 15–20° and watch the velocity graph. Choose a speed that you can easily get again each time, and record it as your target speed.
  3. Release the bob again. When its speed is close to your target, select that section of the force graph and read the maximum force. Record the radius and the force.
  4. Shorten the string, measure the new radius, and repeat, releasing the bob so that it passes through the gate at your target speed. Collect at least 6 readings. Your smallest radius should be less than half your largest.
